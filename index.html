Privacy Policy

Mene: Period & Ovulation App · last updated November 4, 2026

1. Summary

Mene (“the app”) is a private cycle and ovulation journal published by Cool App Helpdesk. You record your cycle, symptoms and signs, and Mene shows estimates for your fertile window, ovulation and next period.

  • We (the developer) never receive your health data. There is no account, no sign-in and no Mene server.
  • Mene has no internet permission. It cannot send your journal anywhere, and it makes no network requests at all.
  • Your journal is encrypted on your device. The encryption key is protected by Android’s Keystore and never leaves the phone.
  • There are no ads, no analytics and no crash-reporting SDKs, and no third party receives your data.
  • If you have Google backup turned on, Android may include an end-to-end encrypted copy of your journal in your own Google account backup, so it survives a new phone. Google cannot read it, and neither can we. You can turn this off in Android settings (section 7).
  • Mene is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition. Its estimates are not a method of contraception.

2. Data we collect

The developer collects nothing. We have no server, the app has no internet permission, and we receive no journal entries, health data, usage data, analytics or identifiers from the app.

The only time we receive anything from you is if you choose to email us for support. In that case we receive your email address and whatever you write in the message, and we use it only to answer you.

Mene is distributed through Google Play. Google Play may collect install and crash information under Google’s Privacy Policy; that is Google’s processing as the app store, not ours.

3. Data stored on your device

Everything you enter stays in the app’s private storage on your phone. The journal is a local database encrypted with SQLCipher; the database key is generated on the device and wrapped by a key in Android’s Keystore. Removing the app or clearing its storage deletes the data.

5. Network activity and third parties

Mene has no network activity. It does not declare the INTERNET permission, so Android prevents it from opening any connection. Your journal, symptoms and estimates are never transmitted.

There is no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, and no social, attribution or cloud-storage SDK. Nothing is sold or shared, because nothing is collected.

Two features can move data, and both are under your control:

  • Export: when you tap “Export my data”, the app writes a JSON file to a location you choose. Nothing is uploaded by Mene; if you then share that file, the app you share it with handles it.
  • Android backup: if your phone has Google backup and a screen lock, Android may back up the encrypted journal to your Google account. It is encrypted on the device before it leaves, using Android’s end-to-end encrypted backup, so Google stores it without being able to read it. Restoring happens through Android when you reinstall or set up a new phone. See Android backup help and Google’s Privacy Policy.

6. Children’s privacy

Mene is intended for adults and for teenagers old enough to manage their own health records. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children — the developer collects no personal information from anyone.

A parent or guardian who believes a child has entered information can remove it by deleting the data in the app or uninstalling it (section 7).

7. Data retention and deletion

Your journal stays on your device until you delete it. In Profile → Delete everything you can erase the whole journal after a confirmation; removing a person in the app erases that person’s journal too. Uninstalling Mene or clearing its storage in Android Settings → Apps → Mene → Storage also deletes everything on the phone.

An exported JSON file is yours and remains wherever you saved it until you delete it. A Google backup copy is kept by Google under your account’s backup settings; you can remove it in Android Settings → Google → Backup or your Google account’s backup page.

The developer holds no copy of your data, so there is nothing for us to retain or delete on a server.

8. Security

The journal is encrypted at rest with SQLCipher. Its random 256-bit key is wrapped by an AES-GCM key held in Android’s Keystore, which cannot be exported from the device. The app never writes the key in plain text or to logs.

Mene also offers an optional app lock, uses Android’s app sandbox, and blocks screenshots and screen recording of the app in the release build. Because the app has no internet permission, there is no network path for the data to leave the phone.

No system is perfectly secure. Keep a screen lock on your phone, and remember that an exported JSON file is not encrypted.
